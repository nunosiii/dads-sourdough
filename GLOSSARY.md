# Glossary

Terms used on the Schedule tab.

**Anchor**: the one moment you pin. The schedule works out every other step from it. Each anchor is either a start or an end.

**Start anchor**: a pinned moment at the beginning of the bake day: feeding the starter, starting the autolyse, or the starter reaching its peak.

**End anchor**: a pinned moment at the end of the bake day: the first loaf going in the oven (Bake), or the bread being ready to eat.

**Starter peak**: the point where the fed starter has risen as high as it will go and is ready to mix in. Also how many hours that takes after feeding.

**Bake**: the first loaf goes into the Dutch oven.

**Ready to eat**: the first loaf has finished its full 2-hour cool and can be sliced. An end anchor.

**Kitchen temp**: the room temperature in °F where the starter and the dough sit. It changes how long the starter peak and the bulk ferment take. It does not change the cold proof (fridge) or the bake (oven). This kitchen runs about 74°F in summer and 68 to 70°F in winter.

**Stage**: a stretch of waiting, such as the starter rising, the bulk ferment, the cold proof or the cool. It has a start, a stop and a length.

**Hands-on step**: something you have to do yourself, such as feeding, mixing, folding, shaping, loading the oven or taking a loaf out. Waiting through a stage is not a hands-on step.

**Night window**: 8:00 PM up to 6:00 AM. A hands-on step that falls in it gets flagged so you are not up late working on the bread.
